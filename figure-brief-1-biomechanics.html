🔧 The Science of Partner Stunt Technique

Partner stunting = one base + one flyer. A stunt stays up when the flyer's weight is stacked over the base's feet — and every lift is built from one pattern: legs → hips → arms.

The big idea: the flyer's weight should travel bone-through-bone from her shoe to the floor. Every joint that's lined up carries weight almost for free. Every joint that's bent pays muscle for every second — that's what a "leaky" stunt is.

The big idea: drift works like a lever — the further out the weight gets, the harder it pries, and it gets worse fast. That's why "a small early step beats a big late one" is physics, not a preference.

The big idea: legs → hips → arms, always. The legs are several times stronger than the arms — a base who starts a lift with his arms picked his weakest muscles for the hardest job. A good dip is shallow, fast, and vertical: think jump, not squat.
FIG 1D

Tight beats strong — why the same flyer can feel light or heavy

Flyer stateWhat happens when the base corrects 2 cmHow the stunt behaves
Tight — ribs down, hips tucked, everything squeezedALL of her moves 2 cm, instantly — his fix worksone solid piece: controllable
Loose — ribs flared, soft hips or kneesThe fix gets swallowed in her joints; her weight lags behinda wobbling stack — that back-and-forth wobble is usually a loose flyer, not a bad base
The base's fix-it ladder, best to worst: tiny pressure changes in the hands → early micro-step → late chase step → ❌ yanking with the arms while the feet stay frozen — that last one usually creates the fall it's trying to stop.

🔍 Finding the Real Cause:
How to Fix Any Stunt

Repping a mistake harder doesn't wear it down — it wears it in. Diagnose before you drill. Here's the method, and the ten faults every pair meets.

Scroll
✉️ Email me this link
How to read this page: Plate 4A is the method — run it on any problem. Plate 4B is the ten most common faults, each with the quick test that finds the real cause. Plate 4C shows where in the video the cause actually lives.
FIG 4A

The checking order — tightness → timing → stack → strength

1

FILM TWO ANGLES, FREEZE AT THE START

Side view = forward-back problems. Front view = side-to-side. Then scrub backward to the FIRST frame where something changed — the cause lives there, not at the crash.

2

CHECK TIGHTNESS FIRST

Is she one solid piece? Cheapest thing to check — and a loose flyer imitates every other problem: weak, off-balance, and bad timing all at once.

3

THEN TIMING

Slow-motion: does her jump finish exactly as his drive peaks? A gap there makes strong pairs feel weak and causes travel that looks like a balance problem.

4

THEN THE STACK

Freeze-frame: is her weight over his hands? Are his hands over his feet? These are the true alignment problems.

5

STRENGTH — LAST, ALWAYS

Blamed first, guilty least often, and the only one you can't fix today. You only get to blame strength after the other three are proven clean.

✓

CHANGE ONE THING → FAIR REPS → RETEST → WRITE IT DOWN

Change three things and you've learned nothing — you don't know which one worked.

FIG 4B

The ten faults — what's really happening, and the quick test

FAULT 1

The stunt drifts backward

Her weight has moved behind centre — heels, or a flared-rib arch that shifts her upper body back. Gravity pries at him non-stop.

🧪 Side view, freeze: hips behind ankles = her. Her line clean but the whole tower leans = his hands.
Fix the weight, not his arms: "ribs to hips" + "toes heavy" (her) · "hands over your toes" (him).
FAULT 2

The extension slowly sinks

Elbows never truly locked, the shrug collapsing with fatigue, or her squeeze fading so she sits into his grip.

🧪 Sinks immediately = never locked (him). Sinks at 4–5 s = fatigue, or her ribs flaring — watch that moment.
Fix: lockout holds + honest pressing numbers (him) · longer hollows + "squeeze tall" mid-hold (her).
FAULT 3

The flyer travels on tosses

Once she leaves his hands, her path is already decided — travel is ALWAYS a launch problem: broken shape, angled push, or a late jump.

🧪 Shape breaks = her. Same direction every rep = his push angle (check his chest in the dip). Direction varies = timing.
Fix: straight-rides only · the wall drill (him) · loads on counts.
FAULT 4

The heavy cradle

The slow-down got crammed into an instant: caught too low, stiff arms and knees, or she opened her shape early and landed loose.

🧪 Ears first — loud IS hard. Then his knees at contact. Then her hips against the peak.
Fix: "meet her high, ride it down" from prep height first (him) · "hold your shape till hands" (her).
FAULT 5

The wobbly liberty

The muscle on the side of her standing hip keeps her pelvis level — if it's weak, the hip drops, weight slides, and the wobble loop begins. Or his feet are frozen.

🧪 THE GROUND TEST: one leg, floor, dead still, 20 seconds. Fails = her conditioning. Passes = film his feet.
Fix: the balance ladder (her) · "quiet hands, busy feet" (him). More wobbling liberties just practice the wobble.
FAULT 6

The heel stretch dumps sideways

She's chasing the lifted leg past what her strength can control, shifting weight off the standing column. Worse if he follows the lean — following teaches leaning.

🧪 THE GROUND GATE: 15-second hold on the floor, that leg. Fails = the stunt was never the problem.
Fix: cap the air shape at ~80% of her ground range and grow it with strength-at-range work.
FAULT 7

Everything feels heavy

Her jump finishes AFTER his drive peaks — she lands on his power instead of adding to it. The pair works double and everyone blames strength. It was never strength.

🧪 Slow-mo, side view: her push should hand off to his arms in one smooth motion. A visible gap = timing.
Fix: counts out loud, loads-to-sponge volume. Nearly 100% fix rate for pairs who do the boring work.
FAULT 8

The base's wrists hurt

Weight hanging on bent-back wrists instead of flat, stacked ones — or a volume spike his wrists couldn't keep up with.

🧪 Look at his grip under a held prep. Bent wrists = mechanics. Flat but painful = check the log for a spike.
Fix: "punch the ceiling," daily wrist care, cut volume until pain-free. Pain that lingers → professional. No hero weeks.
FAULT 9

Fear after a fall

Her body is protecting her: bracing, hesitating, over-gripping — which makes the stunt worse, which "proves" the fear right. It feeds itself.

🧪 You don't test — you notice. She goes quiet, hesitates on "set," starts "forgetting" the skill. QUIET IS FEAR.
Fix: drop TWO stages, rebuild with full spotting — fast guaranteed wins convince the nervous system. Talk the fall through. The timeline belongs to her. Never force the rep.
FAULT 10

The full-up that hits sometimes

A twist on an unstable foundation: she starts it with her head, his release timing varies, or the toss is too low so every tiny error gets magnified.

🧪 Several reps, two angles: head whips early = her. Height varies = his toss (test it plain, no twist). Identical but landings vary = his hands.
Fix: rebuild from the half-up, a quarter turn at a time. "Grow first, then turn."

The habit: scrub backward from the crash to the first frame where something changed. That frame is your answer — everything after it is just consequences.
🛡 The culture that makes all of this work: faults are information, not accusations. Blame athletes for problems and they stop reporting them — no more down-calls, no more "that felt off," no more honest logs. The best fix-it culture and the best safety culture are the same culture.

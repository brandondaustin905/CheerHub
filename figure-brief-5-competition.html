🏆 Competition Science: The Routine as a Designed Object

A partner stunt routine is 45–60 seconds, roughly 6–10 scoring moments, two athletes, nowhere to hide. Judges behave in predictable ways — first impressions anchor, last impressions stick, and falls cost more than hits earn — and the routine below is engineered for exactly that.

The core truth: a dropped elite skill costs more than a hit solid skill earns. With only 6–10 scoring moments, one fall is 10–15% of the whole routine. Chains multiply the risk: five skills that each hit 95% only hit as a connected block about 3 times in 4 — swap one 85% skill into the chain and it drops to about 2 in 3. Sequences get built from your most boringly reliable skills. That's what makes them scoreable.

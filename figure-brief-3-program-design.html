📅 The 8-Week Program:
Gates, Not Calendars

You move forward when you can prove you're ready — not because the calendar says so. The chain below IS the program.

How to read this page: Plate 3A is the whole program as a chain of locked gates — pass one, unlock the next. Plate 3B is what's really happening when a week won't pass. Plate 3C shows how to adapt the program without breaking it.
FIG 3A

The gate chain — pass it to unlock it

PHASE 1 · CALIBRATE (Weeks 1–2)
Calls, hollow work, shoulder sits. Nothing above the shoulders — the pair is learning each other.
✅ GATE 1: 30-second hollow hold · 20-second one-leg balance · 10/10 shoulder sits · calls automatic — and each athlete says they're ready, asked separately
PHASE 2 · PREP MASTERY (Weeks 3–4)
The prep is the laboratory: load timing, micro-step corrections, and cradles graded by ear (a quiet catch is a soft catch).
✅ GATE 2: 10-second motionless prep · 10/10 QUIET cradles · timing so steady the coach can't tell rep 1 from rep 10
PHASE 3 · EXTENSION (Weeks 5–6)
Press to lockout, walking extensions — plus 🛡 FALL SCHOOL in Week 6: practicing the break before Phase 4 makes breaks likely. Train the parachute before the jump.
✅ GATE 3: 8/10 extensions held 5 s · 5-step walking extension · 15-second ground shape holds · fall drill complete — a gate, not a formality
PHASE 4 · THE SINGLE-LEG DOOR (Weeks 7–8)
Extended liberty, then the first shape — but only if the liberty holds 5+ seconds AND her ground gate is passed. Prerequisites, not dates.
🎓 GRADUATION: liberty at 80%+ over LOGGED reps · ALL earlier gates retested (skills fade quietly if you stop checking) · both athletes confident, asked separately
Why gates beat calendars: stunt skills genuinely stack — an extension can't be pressed off a shaky prep, and a liberty can't balance on a shaky platform. Calendars guess how fast a pair adapts; gates measure it. And "93% over 40 logged reps" protects athletes in a way "they look ready" never can.
FIG 3B

The stuck weeks — what's really happening

Stuck at Week 4 — cradle hesitation

It's fear, not technique. Grinding reps on a scared flyer teaches her to brace, which makes cradles worse. Fix: back to low-height fall practice until the catch is trusted — then climb again.

Stuck at Week 5 — the press won't lock out

It's strength. Words don't add kilograms. Fix: honest look at his pressing numbers, add a pressing day, hold the stunt progression one week, retest.

Stuck at Week 7 — the liberty wobbles

Run the ground test first: can she stand one-legged, dead still, for 20 seconds? If not, it's her one-leg conditioning — more liberty attempts just practice the wobble. Fix: the balance ladder. If she passes, watch his feet.

Stuck anywhere — an athlete goes quiet

Quiet is fear. Fix: stop progressing and find out why. No deadline is worth training scared.
The rule over all of it: diagnose before you drill (Figure Brief 4 is the full method). Repeating a week is the system working — repeating honestly beats advancing dishonestly, and the log proves it by graduation.

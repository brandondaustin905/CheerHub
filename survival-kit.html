By cheer parents, for cheer parents. The stuff nobody hands you when you sign up for your first season. Fill it in once — it's saved right in your browser for every competition after.

📖 How To Use This Kit

  1. Use the ☰ Menu button in the top-right corner to jump between tools at any time.
  2. Each tool has editable fields — type directly into them just like a form.
  3. Your entries save automatically as you type, right in this browser.
  4. Use the Print button on any tool to print a clean, nav-free copy — great for the Emergency Contact Card and Pre-Competition Routine.
  5. Use the Reset button on a tool if you want to clear it and start fresh (e.g. for a new competition).

How saving works: this kit saves your information using your browser's local storage — meaning it's saved on this device, in this browser, not to an account or the cloud.

  • ✅ Come back to the same browser on the same device, and everything you typed will still be there.
  • ⚠️ Switching devices or browsers means your entries won't carry over automatically.
  • ⚠️ Clearing your browser's history/cache/site data will erase what's saved here.
  • 💡 For anything important long-term (like the Emergency Contact Card), print a copy or take a screenshot as backup.

🔬 Proven Facts for High-Performance Young Athletes

General sports-nutrition guidelines from pediatric and sports-dietitian sources — not a substitute for individual medical or dietitian advice.

  • Hydration timing: aim for roughly 400–600 mL (about 1.5–2.5 cups) of cold water 2–3 hours before intense training or competing.
  • During activity: small sips every 15-20 minutes helps maintain performance, especially in hot gyms or long competition days.
  • Electrolytes: plain water is enough for most practices under an hour; add electrolytes for sessions over an hour, back-to-back routines, or heavy sweating.
  • Meal timing: eat a full meal at least 3 hours before intense activity; keep snacks to 1-2 hours before.
  • Recovery window: a recovery snack within 30-60 minutes after training (carbs + protein) helps muscles rebuild faster.
  • Protein: young athletes generally need somewhat more protein than non-athletes their age — lean sources spread across the day work better than one big serving.
  • Skip the energy drinks: pediatric sports-medicine sources consistently advise against energy drinks for young athletes — they lack the right electrolyte balance and add excess caffeine/sugar.
  • The competition-day reality: the gap between report time, warm-ups, and your athlete's actual 2-2.5 minute performance is long and unpredictable — often over an hour. Keep a few portable, quick-digesting carb snacks (banana, dry cereal, a granola bar) on hand for the 20 minutes right before warm-ups specifically, not just "sometime during the day."

⚡ Bonus Hack: Pre-pack snacks in labeled bags per day to avoid "hangry chaos" at competitions.

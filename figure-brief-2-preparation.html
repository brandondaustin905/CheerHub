💪 Building the Athletes:
Training & Pair Chemistry

Bases and flyers are two different athletes with two different jobs — and "chemistry" isn't luck. It's three trainable connections, built on a schedule.

Why the push press is the base's #1 lift: it IS the stunt press with a barbell — dip, drive, punch to lockout. Almost everything transfers. And why extra strength matters even for skills he already has: the stronger the base, the easier every rep feels — and easy reps are calm, consistent reps.
FIG 2B

Chemistry, decoded — three trainable connections

CONNECTION 1

TIMING

His drive and her jump peak at the same instant, so their power adds together instead of cancelling out. Trained with: counts and the same call rhythm, forever.
CONNECTION 2

FEEL

He reads her weight-shifts through his hands; she reads his corrections through her feet. Trained with: gentle nudge drills and low eyes-closed holds.
CONNECTION 3

KNOWING EACH OTHER

How she stiffens when he steps; how his dip feels the moment before launch. Trained with: shared reps. Lots of them. There is no shortcut.
THE FRAME AROUND ALL THREE — the down-call rule: anyone can call any stunt down, any time, and nobody argues. A pair runs on honest information — an athlete afraid to say "I'm off" hides the exact signal the whole system needs.

The training week — keeping stunting and conditioning out of each other's way

DayWhat happensWhy this order
1 — New skillshardest material, fresh, fully spotted, 8–12 quality repsnew skills are learned with attention, not volume — tired reps teach the fail
2 — Consistencyestablished skills; every rep logged hit/missthis is where routine-readiness is built
3 — Conditioningeach role trains its system (Fig 2A) — no max stuntingtired catches are slow catches
Daily × 10 minhollow work, calls, wrist/ankle prepsmall daily deposits beat weekly cramming
The season in three moves: ☀ Summer — BUILD (new skills, heaviest training) → 🍂 Fall — LOCK IT IN (percentages up, list locks at 90%) → ❄ Competition — PROTECT (volume down, nothing new enters; changes only remove skills). The recovery floor, always: sleep, enough food, and any pain that lingers gets seen by a professional — never trained through.

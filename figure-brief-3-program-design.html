The 8-Week Program runs on GATES: you move forward when you can PROVE you're ready, and not one day before. Why that matters so much in stunting: - STUNT SKILLS GENUINELY STACK. An extension is pressed off a prep — if the prep can't hold still at chin height, there is nothing stable to press FROM. A liberty balances on a platform — if the platform wobbles, her one-leg control never gets a fair chance. These aren't traditions; they're how the skills are physically built. - CALENDARS GUESS. GATES MEASURE. A calendar assumes every pair adapts at the average speed. No pair is average — a strong base with a brand-new flyer, a veteran flyer with a developing base, every combination moves differently. Gates let each pair move at their true speed: sometimes faster than the calendar, sometimes slower, always honestly. - GATES PROTECT ATHLETES FROM PRESSURE. "They look ready" is an opinion — and opinions bend when a competition is coming or a parent is watching. "They're at 93% over 40 logged reps" is a fact, and facts don't bend. The checkpoint list turns readiness into something you can point to. The standing rules, and what each one is really for: - A failed checkpoint repeats the week → the program bends; the standards don't. - Every session gets logged → the log is what makes the gates real instead of vibes. - Pain beyond normal soreness pauses the plan → eight weeks means eight HEALTHY weeks. - Experienced pairs can skip to Week 3 only by PASSING the Week 2 checkpoint on day one → you enter on evidence, not on your résumé. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.2 PHASE 1 (WEEKS 1–2): WHAT "CALIBRATE" IS REALLY BUILDING On paper, Phase 1 looks almost too easy — ground drills, shoulder sits, nothing above the shoulders. Here's what's actually being built: - THE CALLS BECOME AUTOMATIC. The "set–dip" rhythm is the pair's shared clock (Chapter 2). Two weeks of saying it on every single rep wires it in before any skill depends on it. - HE LEARNS HER WEIGHT; SHE LEARNS HIS HANDS. Shoulder sits are chosen precisely because they're low-risk with lots of contact time — maximum learning about each other, minimum consequence. - HER TIGHTNESS GETS INSTALLED FIRST. Statue drills and hollow holds build the one-solid-piece body BEFORE it's ever needed at height, where the price of looseness goes up. - ENDING EVERY SESSION WITH 10 PERFECT SHOULDER SITS is deliberate. It feels boring. Boring is the point — trust is built out of predictable, successful reps, stacked daily. WHY THE WEEK 2 CHECKPOINT ASKS WHAT IT ASKS: the 30-second hollow and 20-second one-leg balance are the minimum body-control every Phase 2 skill will lean on. The 10/10 shoulder sits prove the timing is real. And asking each athlete SEPARATELY whether they're ready matters, because athletes perform confidence for their partners — especially flyers for their bases. Ask them alone and you get the truth. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.3 PHASE 2 (WEEKS 3–4): THE PREP IS THE LABORATORY The prep is every stunt skill in miniature — the load timing, the stacked support, the drift corrections, the dismount — all at reduced height and reduced consequence. Phase 2 trains the pair's entire mechanical vocabulary at the cheapest price it will ever be sold for. The details that matter: - THE LOAD IS TAUGHT SLOW BEFORE FAST. Loading is a timing skill; you add speed to accuracy, never accuracy to speed. - 8–12 QUALITY ATTEMPTS, THEN STOP. New skills are learned with attention, and attention runs out before muscles do. Reps past that point don't just fail — they practice the failure. - THE MICRO-STEP DRILL (coach drifts the flyer; base may only fix it with his feet) exists to build the right habit before the wrong one shows up. The arm-yank-with-frozen-feet mistake from Chapter 1 gets blocked here, in advance. - CRADLES ARRIVE WITH THE SOUND TEST. Quiet catch = soft catch. The pair learns to grade their own landings by ear from day one — a built-in coach that never misses a rep. WHY THE WEEK 4 CHECKPOINT ASKS WHAT IT ASKS: a 10-second motionless prep proves the platform the extension will be pressed from. QUIET cradles prove the landing mechanics before height multiplies the forces. "Never grabs the base's head in a wobble" proves her panic responses have been retrained. And "the coach can't tell rep 1 from rep 10" proves the timing is genuinely automatic, not occasionally lucky. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.4 PHASE 3 (WEEKS 5–6): STRENGTH MEETS SKILL — AND FALL SCHOOL The press to extension is the program's first true strength test: leg power travelling through the whole body to a locked-out finish overhead. It's also where drift gets more expensive — the same lean at extension height pries harder than it did at prep (the lever problem from Chapter 1). That's exactly why the platform was certified stable first. Built into these weeks: - LOWERING IS A SKILL TOO. Returning from extension to prep gets trained on purpose, not just survived. - THE CONDITIONING MATCHES THE MOMENT. Lockout holds and overhead carries show up in the gym the same weeks the extension demands them — the body gets built for the job as the job arrives. - THE SHAPE GATE OPENS EARLY. The 15-second ground holds for heel stretch and arabesque start banking NOW, two weeks before shapes fly — so Phase 4 never stalls waiting on flexibility. - FALL SCHOOL (Week 6, Day 3) — THE MOST IMPORTANT 15 MINUTES OF THE PROGRAM. Deliberate low-height practice of exactly what to do when a stunt breaks: her bail positions, his chest-to-chest absorb, the spotter's save. Falling well is a skill, and skills get learned when everyone is calm — not improvised mid-emergency. It's scheduled immediately BEFORE Phase 4 because Phase 4 is where falls become likely. You train the parachute before the jump. WHY THE WEEK 6 CHECKPOINT ASKS WHAT IT ASKS: 8-of-10 extensions held 5 seconds is the stability the liberty will stand on. Quiet cradles from height re-prove the landings at bigger forces. The 5-step walking extension proves he can keep the platform under her while moving. And the fall drill is a gate, not a formality — nobody enters Phase 4 without it. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.5 PHASE 4 (WEEKS 7–8): PRECISION — AND GRADUATION The extended liberty cuts her support in half and hands the balance job to the side of her standing hip, while he learns to read all of her weight through a single foot. It's the program's precision test. The design choices: - LOW VOLUME, TOTAL ATTENTION. 6–10 attempts, every one debriefed. This is the most information-dense skill in the program — you learn it with your head as much as your legs. - "BANK A WIN AFTER A FIGHT." Any liberty that wobbles hard is followed by two guaranteed extensions. The session's last impressions become confidence deposits, on purpose. - THE CONDITIONING TURNS WITH THE SKILL. Week 7 is her one-leg emphasis week and his anti-twist week — because that's precisely what the liberty exposes. - THE FIRST SHAPE IS CONDITIONAL. Heel stretch attempts happen only if the liberty holds 5+ seconds AND her ground gate is passed. One scheduling decision that shows the whole program's philosophy: the shape waits for its prerequisites, not for the calendar. - TESTING DAY IS ITS OWN DAY. Measurement is separated from training — unhurried, formal, logged. And Day 3 closes the block with a Week-1-vs-Week-8 film comparison. Show the pair their own progress; it's usually shocking, and visible progress is fuel. WHY GRADUATION RETESTS EVERYTHING: skills fade quietly if you stop checking them. Retesting every previous gate proves the pair KEPT what they built, not just touched it once. Add the 80%+ logged liberty, the separate confidence interviews, and a log whose numbers are climbing week over week — and graduation means something. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.6 THE STUCK WEEKS: WHAT'S REALLY HAPPENING Most pairs repeat at least one week. That's the system working. What matters is fixing the RIGHT thing: - STUCK AT WEEK 4 (cradle hesitation): it's almost never technique — it's fear. Grinding more cradle reps on a scared flyer teaches her body to brace, which makes cradles worse. The fix runs backward: low-height fall practice until the catch is trusted, then climb again. - STUCK AT WEEK 5 (the press won't lock out): it's strength. No cue on earth fixes a base who's pressing at his limit — words don't add kilograms. The fix: an honest look at his pressing numbers, an extra pressing day, hold the stunt progression a week, retest. Pressing strength comes fast when you train it directly. - STUCK AT WEEK 7 (the liberty wobbles): run the ground test first — can she stand one-legged, dead still, 20 seconds? If not, it's her one-leg conditioning, and more liberty attempts just practice the wobble. If she passes, watch his feet. - STUCK ANYWHERE (an athlete goes quiet): stop progressing and find out why. Quiet is fear, and no deadline is worth training scared. The rule over all of it: DIAGNOSE BEFORE YOU DRILL (Chapter 4 is the full method). Repeating a week honestly beats advancing dishonestly — and the log will prove it by graduation. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 3.7 ADAPTING THE PROGRAM WITHOUT BREAKING IT - EXPERIENCED PAIRS: enter at Week 3 by passing Gate 1 on day one. The gate moves; the gating never does. - REBUILDING PAIRS (after a fall or time off): run the whole chain, faster. Phases can shorten; they cannot be skipped, because trust and timing rebuild in the same order they were built. - MISMATCHED PAIRS (a veteran with a rookie): the PAIR moves at the slower partner's speed — the connection between them can't be rushed by one person's résumé. The veteran's own conditioning can keep progressing separately. - DIFFERENT SCHEDULES: the three-day structure can be rearranged as long as three things survive: new skills happen FRESH, consistency reps get LOGGED, and heavy lifting never lands on max-stunting days. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ WORDS WORTH KNOWING Gate (a checkpoint you must pass, not a date you must reach) · the prep laboratory (full stunt mechanics at the cheapest price) · fall school (practicing the break before it happens) · bank a win (ending a fight with guaranteed successes) · the ground test (20-second one-leg stand — her readiness told on the floor) · diagnose before you drill (find the real cause before repping) · the three survivals (fresh new-skill work, logged consistency, no lifting on max days) CHECK YOURSELF 1.

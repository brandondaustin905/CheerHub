🏆 Competing:
How Judges Score & How Routines Win

45–60 seconds. Roughly 6–10 scoring moments. Two athletes, nowhere to hide. Judges score in predictable ways — and a winning routine is engineered for exactly that.

FIG 5A

The four-beat routine on a 45-second clock

0 s~10 s~34 s45 s
BEAT 1 · THE OPENING STATEMENT

Set the judges' expectations

The first skill tells the panel what kind of pair they're watching — and buys (or costs) benefit-of-the-doubt on every close call after it.

The rule: open with your most impressive GUARANTEED skill. Not your hardest. Those are different skills.
BEAT 2 · THE SEQUENCE BLOCK

Pack in the difficulty

Connected skills — mount into shape into dismount, no resets — read as harder, because connecting removes the rest moments.

The chain rule: five 95% skills chained only survive about 3 runs in 4 — chains are built from your most boringly reliable skills only. 90% material lives alone, where a wobble stays contained.
BEAT 3 · THE PEAK

Put the risk where it costs least

The hardest skill lands after judges are invested, before fatigue peaks, and late enough that a miss can't shadow the whole routine.

The rule: ONE reach skill maximum (85–90%), and it travels with a rehearsed escape plan — a save that looks like choreography. A pair with a plan fears the skill less.
BEAT 4 · THE FINISH

Own the last image

A dismount at your legal max — caught quiet, stuck, and HELD for a full beat. The final picture is what judges carry into the score; a stuck finish cleans up small wobbles in their memory.

The rule: never end on the peak skill's landing. The last picture should be a chosen picture.
Pacing rules: no dead time (unscored seconds are points on the floor) · build rest INTO held skills — a held extension scores and recovers at the same time · build a 40-second routine for a 45-second cap (adrenaline runs long) · music follows the pair's load rhythm, never fights it.
FIG 5B

Why the 90% rule is math, not caution

ROUTINE A — 8 solid skills, each hits 95%

Most runs: a clean sheet, maybe one wobble. The judges see confidence from the first skill to the held finish.

ROUTINE B — 10 elite skills, each hits 80%

Most runs: about 2 drops. Each drop loses three ways — the deduction, the credit that never landed, and the doubt over everything after it.

ROUTINE A WINS — ALMOST EVERY TIME, ON ALMOST EVERY SHEET
The core truth: a dropped hard skill costs more than a hit solid skill earns — and with only 6–10 scoring moments, one fall is 10–15% of the entire routine. Pick skills by what they'll score across ten runs, not what they could score on the perfect one.

THE WRITTEN WARM-UP

Exact skills, exact order, ending on the opener hit clean. Warm-up fires the patterns — it tests nothing. No "one more" after a clean rep.

2

THE 30-MINUTE RULE

Inside the final window, nothing changes. A late change trades a known 90% for an unknown anything. Never worth it.

3

NERVES → "SET… DIP"

All attention goes to the first call of the first skill. The rhythm the pair protected for months is a portable piece of home — and the swap is practiced in run-throughs, not invented on the day.

4

AFTERWARD, EVERY TIME

Collect the sheet → score check with your video (usually a 20–30 minute window — free points live there) → log the performance like any other practice.

🔧 The Biomechanics of Partner Stunt Technique

Partner stunting = one base + one flyer. A stunt stands when the pair's combined centre of mass (COM) stays over the base's base of support (BOS) — and every skill is built from one motor pattern: legs → hips → arms.

Read it in one glance: force should travel bone-through-bone from the flyer's shoe to the floor. Every in-line joint transmits load nearly free; every out-of-line joint converts body-weight support into a continuous muscle problem — the mechanical definition of a "leaky" stunt.

Torque = force × moment arm, and the relationship is linear — a correction at 2 cm costs less than half of one at 5 cm. "A small early step beats a big late one" is physics, not preference.

Sequencing rule: legs → hips → arms, always. The legs are several times stronger than the shoulder girdle — a base who initiates with his arms has chosen his weakest engine. Dip quality = shallow + fast + vertical.

Balance correction hierarchy (base): in-hand pressure → early micro-step (2–5 cm) → late chase step → ❌ arm-yank with frozen feet — that last one creates the fall it's trying to fix.
